SIGNALWATCH

Privacy & support

SignalWatch is developed by Leo the Lion. Contact leolinnz@gmail.com with privacy, support or data deletion requests. Effective 9 November 2026.

What the watch app does

SignalWatch displays headlines and summaries from your chosen Signal server, lets you listen to unread news read aloud, and lets you save stories and mark them as read, and automatically marks news as read when its narration finishes, and can open a story or your briefing in Signal on your paired phone. Demo mode uses sample content stored in the app and does not contact the Signal server.

Listen later queue

Continue on phone/watch can transfer the remaining Listen later queue after explicit acceptance on the receiving device. The source pauses and confirms its latest section and queue revision before receiver playback. The temporary handoff snapshot includes the story text, order, publisher image links and saved sections. It expires five minutes after confirmation and is not a separate permanent history. Expired handoff records are removed from bounded server memory on subsequent handoff requests or a server restart. The receiver verifies the current account queue before starting; conflicting edits or failed verification stop the transfer. Older clients and servers continue to support single-story handoffs but cannot silently accept a queue as one story.

Listen later stores up to ten stories (100,000 text characters in total), their order, publisher image links and saved speech sections in your Signal account. Phone and watch sync while open or while the watch player runs. Story text is encrypted on the watch and cached in browser storage for offline listening with a device voice. Browser storage clears on sign-out. After an online visit, Signal caches only its public app shell and static assets so the phone can reopen an offline queue screen. Account/API responses are not cached; cached account names do not grant an authenticated session. The offline screen can remove its local copy without changing the server queue. Images are not downloaded into the offline queue. You can explicitly download recorded audio for queued stories using your selected Signal reading voices. This sends the same story text to the existing Signal speech service. Audio downloads remain on the device where you request them; handoffs transfer queue text and positions, not audio files. Watch recordings and their index are encrypted using Android Keystore and excluded from backup. Browser recordings are stored locally in IndexedDB under the current account and may be removed by browser storage management. Both devices limit downloaded audio to 100 MB, provide cancel/retry and remove controls, and clear it on sign-out or removal of the local offline copy. By default audio is removed when stories leave the queue; you can turn that cleanup off. Phone browser downloads require Signal to remain open. Watch downloads now use Android WorkManager and resume persisted requests after ordinary app and device restarts, once Android permits work. Automatic mode is off until enabled for the signed-in Watch account; it checks for queued stories on unmetered Watch Wi-Fi while charging, normally using a 15-minute minimum periodic interval whose actual timing Android controls. Manual requests can also require these conditions. HTTP and DNS use the job’s selected network. Account-bound request intent, manual story links and the automatic-mode choice are encrypted; WorkManager stores only opaque request identifiers and numeric progress, not story text, URLs or cookies. Notifications provide progress and cancellation when notification permission is granted. Cancelling or removing downloaded audio disables automatic mode and invalidates pending requests; sign-out clears the schedule and downloaded media. Storage or malformed-data failures pause automatic attempts until you retry; previously authorized offline queue edits sync through the existing idempotent compare-and-swap protocol before downloads, with conflicts keeping the newer server queue; authentication failures disable automatic mode. Temporary network or speech-service failures retry with backoff, retaining completed sections. Android quotas, power management and force-stop can delay work; a force-stopped app must be opened again. Downloads are split into bounded batches. No download starts playback. You can always keep using the offline device voice without downloading audio. Completing or removing a queued story removes its text from the shared queue; a version marker remains so an old offline device cannot restore it. Conflicting offline edits keep the newer server queue and show a notice. Sync does not start playback. Queue records are stored under a hashed account filename in the server’s App_Data/listening-queue directory; there is no automatic expiry. For account-data deletion, remove that record after revoking the account sessions.

Sign-in and account information

Live mode requires an existing Signal account. You sign in on your phone and explicitly approve a short-lived watch code. The watch does not ask for or store your password. After approval, it receives a server-issued account session cookie. The cookie is encrypted on the watch using a device-protected Android Keystore key. Account sessions identify the approving Signal account and allow access to its briefing, saved stories and read status.

Data sent to your server

The app connects over HTTPS to the server you select. Requests include account-session credentials, temporary pairing codes or device secrets, story passages requested for speech narration, and the article links and bookmark/read actions you choose. The server may also process your account email and user identifier, network address, request timestamps and operational logs needed to run and protect the service. Bookmarks, read status and existing account information are stored on the Signal server. The watch stores the server address and encrypted session locally. It keeps the last downloaded briefing and loaded saved stories, up to ten opened or explicitly downloaded supported full articles, and pending bookmark/read actions encrypted on the device. Pending actions sync to the same account/server when connected. These downloads clear on sign-out or account/server replacement.

Watch-to-phone listening

Continue on phone sends the current full-story text, title, source link and speech-section position to your Signal server. A transfer is readable only by the same signed-in account. The phone must explicitly accept and the watch must acknowledge pausing before the claiming browser receives playable text. Pending transfers are valid for five minutes, accepted transfers have a 45-second acknowledgement deadline, and confirmed text remains available for five minutes. Transfers are held in bounded server memory and pruned on subsequent transfer requests or server restart; they are not added to the account database. Only an opaque transfer ID appears in the phone link. The phone tab keeps an opaque ID and claim token for optional sign-in and retries; closing the transfer clears these tab entries. Story text is held in the page’s memory rather than browser storage. Normal Signal narration processes the transferred text under the existing speech policy. Continue on watch can explicitly transfer a full-story snapshot in the reverse direction; full-story listening progress can now sync through the shared Resume feature.

Phone-to-watch listening and news images

Continue on watch sends the full-story title, URL, source, exact narration sections, current section and available image URL through the same short-lived, account-bound transfer service. The open watch app discovers pending offers and requires explicit acceptance. The phone pauses and acknowledges its latest section before the watch receives playable text. Keep both apps open during this transfer. The handoff does not start the watch in the background. Shared full-story Resume is described below. The existing encrypted saved-position snapshot retains transferred text for manual resume.

When you open a story, available HTTPS images download directly from publisher image hosts without Signal session cookies or a referrer. The publisher or its image hosting provider receives normal connection information, including the network address and requested image URL. Requests reject local/private destinations, restrict redirects, image formats, download sizes and decoding dimensions. Images use a bounded temporary memory cache and are not downloaded as part of an offline briefing. Image URLs remain with encrypted story metadata until that metadata is cleared. Missing images leave the news text available. No advertising or image analytics service is added.

Shared listening progress

Full-story playback saves one account-wide snapshot containing the title, publisher link, source, optional image URL, exact narration sections and current section. It is retained in protected Signal server application data until replaced, completed or forgotten, and survives server restarts and deployments. The server keeps a minimal version marker after clearing text so old offline updates cannot recreate a cleared position. Account deletion requests include this data. This feature adds no third-party sync provider.

The watch encrypts its shared snapshot and pending offline updates, scoped to the current server/account and excluded from device backups. The phone website stores its snapshot and pending updates in account-scoped browser local storage so they survive a reload or lost connection; these local entries clear on sign-out. Do not share a signed-in browser profile. Updates retry while the phone page is open, while the watch app or full-story listening service can connect, and on the next open. Conflicting old offline updates retain the newer server position rather than overwriting it. Completion and Forget clear saved text on connected devices when they next sync, without remotely stopping already playing audio. Resume always requires an explicit tap. Briefing queues remain device-local. Operational backup retention also applies to server data.

Continue listening and volume

The watch encrypts the text of your last unfinished listening queue, its current story and speech section, completion history and save time. This is scoped to the same server/account, excluded from backups, and removed on sign-out or account/server replacement. Stop retains progress for manual resume, queue completion clears it, and Forget saved position deletes it explicitly. It can resume offline using an installed local watch voice. Audio does not start automatically after a restart. Online narration still uses your Signal server; no additional third-party service is introduced. Volume buttons use Android media volume settings and the normal MODIFY_AUDIO_SETTINGS permission; they do not request access to your microphone, change alarm/notification settings or apply a saved volume on resume.

Catch me up

Catch me up creates a short extractive briefing from loaded unread headlines and summaries, grouping similar headlines and preserving your personalised feed order. The last prepared briefing and fingerprints of up to 200 completed story versions are encrypted on the watch and scoped to your server and account. Completed segments are remembered without automatically marking stories as read. Skipped and interrupted segments remain eligible. Replay uses the cached briefing; offline narration uses the installed watch voice. This cache and history clear on sign-out or account/server replacement. This feature adds no new third-party service; online narration uses the existing Signal speech endpoint.

Opening news on your phone

When you choose Open on phone, the watch passes a Signal link containing that story's address, headline and source to your own paired phone over the watch connection. Your phone then opens it in Signal or its browser, which contacts your Signal server as the website normally does. The watch does not send this link to the developer or any other party.

Voice search, briefing tile and optional alerts

The notification preview adds voice search through the system speech recognizer. Its provider handles your speech under its own settings; SignalWatch receives recognized text and searches the stories already loaded on the watch. It does not directly record your microphone. The optional Signal Listen later tile shows locally saved queue titles, sections, completed recorded-audio counts and download status. It reads existing account-bound encrypted data on the watch, makes no network requests and never starts audio or schedules downloads by being viewed. Queue opens the controls; Play or Resume starts the current saved queue only after an explicit tap. Playback taps from a previously signed-in account or server are rejected. The briefing tile and optional watch-face complication share an encrypted headline, unread count and load time locally. They show the last refreshed briefing and do not poll for news. The optional Signal markets watch-face complication and Markets & Deals tile store only the last-refreshed stock quote and top tracked Product Watch deal badge encrypted on the watch and clear on sign-out. The optional Signal Companion Android home-screen widget similarly caches only the latest unread count, lead headline, pinned portfolio quote, and top deal locally encrypted on your phone and clears on sign-out. Optional selective alerts send chosen topics and keywords to your Signal server and store them with your account; quiet hours, news limits and notification switches stay on the watch and optionally sync quiet hours with your Signal account.

Optional phone/watch notifications use Firebase Cloud Messaging. Firebase processes an installation identifier, token and notification contents. Signal stores the installation token, account association, optional linked browser endpoint and expiring registration lease. The watch receives alerts through the Wear OS data layer, checks the account and origin, and stores an encrypted account fingerprint and verification time. Disaster reports use the last location and thresholds already configured in Signal on your phone. The watch stores only its latest received disaster report, encrypted and account-bound; sign-out clears this report and the briefing tile. Weather location is separate from these disaster settings. Android notification controls and your own Wear OS forwarding settings also apply. These reports can be delayed or incomplete; follow local authorities.

Permissions and third parties

This release requests internet access, network status and optional notification permission. Screen-off listening uses a media-playback foreground service and a partial wake lock while playing or preparing; the media controls let you pause or stop it. Narration recordings remain temporary. Offline listening uses the installed watch voice. It has no advertising or analytics SDK, makes no in-app purchases, and requests no health, contacts, camera or microphone permissions. Watch weather optionally requests precise or approximate location while its screen is open. The current Signal Companion requests approximate location. QR codes are generated locally. The phone's own camera can scan a code; SignalWatch does not access that camera. Network infrastructure and the hosting provider process traffic needed to deliver the service. The default service uses Tailscale Funnel for HTTPS hosting. If you select another Signal server, its operator and privacy practices may differ. Any Google sign-in occurs on the Signal website, under the policies of that service and Google.

Local weather

SignalWatch and Signal Companion offer optional current conditions and a seven-day forecast without requiring account sign-in. SignalWatch 0.14.1 can request a fresh high-accuracy location fix using GPS when precise location is allowed; approximate access remains available. Existing approximate-location users can choose Use precise location in Local weather. The current Signal Companion uses approximate location. When you open or refresh weather with location permission, the device sends the permitted coordinates, rounded to four decimal places, directly to Open-Meteo over HTTPS. Open-Meteo also receives connection information such as your IP address. No Signal account credentials are sent to the weather provider. Weather is held in memory; the apps do not keep a location history or request background location. Denying weather permission leaves news and notifications available. Revoke location access in Android app settings. Weather does not update your separately configured disaster-alert location.

Retention and your choices

Pairing codes expire after ten minutes or are consumed once. Server restarts invalidate outstanding codes. Sign-out clears the watch's saved session; uninstalling removes its app data. Neither action deletes your server account. Signal's server retains account information while the account exists; unbookmarked story history follows the server's configured retention period and saved stories remain until removed. Operational backups are retained separately and may keep previously stored information until rotated; ask the operator about the applicable backup schedule.

Request access or deletion

For the default service, email leolinnz@gmail.com from your account address and describe your request. We may verify account ownership before removing account data. Do not send your password. For a custom server, contact its operator. See the data request instructions.
